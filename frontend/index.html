Validation & Method

Verified in the Jamnagar pilot, Gujarat. Labels were hand-checked against satellite imagery there, and only there. Labels in every other state are unvalidated.

What the map shows

Every VIIRS 375 m thermal detection over India from NASA FIRMS (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21), the full period on record. Live near-real-time detections are added every 6 hours. Times are shown in IST.

How each detection is labelled

Fixed rules, not a trained model. Each detection gets a label only when exactly one rule matches:

Gas flare
Within 1 km of a flare-capable Global Energy Monitor (GEM) facility and active on at least 5 days at the same ~375 m cell.
Gas flare (offshore)
In the Mumbai High zone, at least 10 km beyond India's boundary, and active on at least 5 days at the same ~375 m cell (counted over the whole offshore history). Other offshore detections, and every KG basin detection, stay unclassified. No land data is used.
Industrial
Recurring heat (2+ days) within 2 km of GEM heat-industry facilities, or inside a mapped OSM industrial site; a one-day detection inside an OSM industrial site or within 500 m of heat industry; or recurring heat near a coal mine. Solar and wind plants never count as industry.
Agricultural burning
ESA WorldCover cropland (class 40), more than 2 km from any mapped industrial site.
Wildfire
WorldCover tree cover, shrubland or grassland (classes 10/20/30), more than 2 km from any mapped industrial site.
Unclassified — needs review
No rule matched, or more than one did. This is not a fire type; these detections need manual review and are left out of model training.

Offshore gas flares. The India boundary used to clip these out. Two zones (Mumbai High, lon 70.5–72.3 / lat 18.5–20.5; KG basin, lon 81.0–83.5 / lat 14.5–17.5) now keep detections 10+ km beyond the boundary, shown as the “Offshore” region and counted in every total. The 5-day threshold was fixed before checking and is the onshore flare threshold. A Sentinel-2 SWIR check found a hot pixel within 200 m at 21 of 30 randomly drawn recurring cells (95% range 52–83%) and at 0 of 15 open-sea points (0–20%); the pass rule was at least 40% and at most 1 of 15. That draw held one KG basin cell (29 of 30 were Mumbai High). A follow-up on all six recurring KG basin cells found a hot pixel at 1 of 4 that had two clear scenes, so, as a conservative change made after seeing it, KG basin detections are shown but never labelled gas flare.

Fire intensity (FRP) is never used to assign a label. Day/night is also left out on purpose so it can serve as an independent sanity check. Anomalous means the detection's FRP is a robust outlier (one-sided modified z-score > 3.5) against the same site's earlier days, and only for sites with at least 5 earlier active days.

Industrial types (Refinery / oil & gas, Thermal power plant, Mine / coal-seam fire, Steel, Cement, Chemical / petrochemical) are display-only. Each names the nearest mapped facility of a known type within 1 km, from OpenStreetMap and Global Energy Monitor's 1,166-site multi-sector table. The labelling rules still read only GEM's 98-site table, so types never change a label. The optional “Colour by: Industrial type” map mode has its own blue / cyan / violet palette; class colours are unchanged in the default Class mode. The type describes the facility nearby, not necessarily the source of the fire. About a quarter of industrial detections have no typed facility that close and read “Industrial, type not identified”. Counts include live detections through the latest data, so they exceed the historical-only build (132,698 industrial detections).

Types are mostly unvalidated. They were compared with hand notes on 67 verified-industrial rows, almost all Reliance and Nayara refineries: 53 of 54 refinery notes agreed. Steel (0 of 1), cement (1 of 1) and thermal power (0 of 3, all plants inside refineries) have almost no test rows, and mine and chemical types have none. Treat steel, cement, mine, thermal power and chemical types as unvalidated.

  • Industrial: of the 12 independent hold-out detections the rules labelled industrial, 11 were verified industrial; the 12th was a gas flare.
  • Wildfire: all 5 misses were crop burning on land ESA WorldCover maps as shrubland.
  • Gas flares: all 11 verified flares were detected and classified as industrial; separating flares from other process heat inside refineries isn't possible at 375 m (5 methods tested, below).

The 95% ranges are Wilson score intervals. With samples this small they are wide: the true accuracy could be anywhere in the range. An earlier 69-row review is not counted here, because it was used to fix the rules and so can't also measure them.

Tested and rejected

Five ways to tell a gas flare from ordinary refinery heat, tested on the 11 visually confirmed Jamnagar flares. None worked well enough to adopt:

  • GEM facility distance (still the rule, since nothing better was found): caught 0 of 11 flares. The GEM point sits 1.9–3.4 km from the stacks.
  • Refinery polygons: caught 7 of 11 flares, but overall agreement fell (66.7% vs 73.3% on the hold-out set) because it calls ordinary refinery heat a flare.
  • OSM flare proximity (500 m): caught 7 of 11 flares, but 17 of 45 verified industrial rows were also within 500 m of a mapped flare.
  • VIIRS brightness (I4/I5, FRP, day/night): the flare I4 median is higher (326.5 K vs 310.0 K) but the distributions overlap heavily. The other measures show no separation.
  • Sentinel-2 SWIR hot pixels: found at 63.6% of flares vs 55.6% of industrial sites, which is no real separation at n = 11.

Also rejected:

  • Brick-kiln rule (within 500 m of a mapped kiln): 3 of 7 decided rows correct (3 more uncertain), so it was removed.

Not yet validated

  • Everywhere outside the Jamnagar pilot area. Coverage of OpenStreetMap industrial sites also varies a lot between states.
  • Wildfire labels that rest on shrubland: 7.25% nationally, but concentrated in Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka, where they make up 38.1% and 51.7% of wildfire labels. If the Gujarat pattern holds there, many of these may be crop burning.
  • Wildfire labels on grassland (18.6% nationally) have not been tested.
  • Every alert threshold is an untested starting value.

Known limits

  • A 375 m pixel over a refinery can contain a flare stack and ordinary process heat together.
  • Known facilities come from GEM and OpenStreetMap. A heat source missing from both cannot be labelled industrial.
